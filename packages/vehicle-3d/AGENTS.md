# @repo/vehicle-3d Agent Instructions

Three.js vehicle viewer (BMW X5 G05) as a JIT internal package: no build step, `exports` map straight to `src/`. It is **not** part of the design system (`@repo/ui`) and not a shadcn registry item.

## Where `three` may be imported

`three` and `@react-three/fiber` are **dependencies of this package only**. `@repo/ui`, `apps/web`, and `apps/docs` must not import them directly; they use this package:

`'use client'` everywhere. An app must load the viewer client-only (`next/dynamic` with `ssr: false`) and show a fallback while it loads. **Never put Motion on the WebGL canvas**; animate colour or camera inside three (`useFrame` + `invalidate`) or with GSAP on a non-canvas element. Dispose geometries/materials you replace.

## Assets are not in this package

`VehicleCanvas` fetches `/models/<file>` and `/models/studio.hdr` from the host app. The host decides how they get there:

- Storybook: `pnpm --filter @repo/ui copy:car` downloads the BMW X5 G05 parts (CC BY 4.0, BMW Car IT) and the Poly Haven HDR (CC0) into `packages/ui/.storybook/public/models/` (gitignored). `build-storybook` removes `storybook-static/models/` afterwards (`packages/ui/scripts/prune-storybook-models.mjs`, fails if any 3D asset remains), so meshes and the HDR never ship in a static Storybook.
- Apps ship the **optimized** model: `apps/web/public/models/g05-web/*.glb` (13 parts, 6.5MB, meshopt-compressed, **no emblem part**) with its `LICENSE.txt`, plus `public/models/studio.hdr` (CC0). Regenerate with `pnpm --filter @repo/vehicle-3d optimize:g05` (reads the `copy:car` originals and writes straight into that folder; Storybook serves the same folder via `staticDirs`, so the `x5-web` stories need no extra step). The vehicle id is `x5-web`; `x5` is the original for Storybook only. Third-party terms live in the root `NOTICE`. Never commit the 40MB originals.
- `VehicleCanvas` loads `.glb` with `GLTFLoader` + `MeshoptDecoder` (inlined in three, no extra files).

## Camera views

Presets live in `src/camera-views.ts` in the fitted world frame (car about 4.2 long, **front is +Z, the steering wheel is on +X**, ground y=-0.62; `x5` and `x5-web` share it). `exterior` and `wheel` orbit a target with distance limits. `seats` (between the front seats, looking back) and `dashboard` (driver eye, looking forward) are **inside**: the aim point is pulled 0.1 in front of the camera so a drag looks around in place, there is no zoom, and pitch is limited (26° up, 11° down from horizontal; note OrbitControls' polar angle is the camera's angle seen from the aim point, so looking down is `minPolarAngle`) because more makes the picture tilt, and past about 17° down the `seats` view shows the flat black inside of the cargo area filling the top third. While the camera moves, limits are opened and input is off, then the destination's limits apply. Inside, a point light and a higher `scene.environmentIntensity` are blended in (`CABIN_LIGHT`) because the roof shades the sun and the black interior otherwise reads as silhouettes; it is still dark by nature (black leather) and the white door trims are already near saturation, so do not raise it further without looking at both. The interior is the model's default: the selected seat and interior colours do not change it, and hosts must say so. `near` is 0.02 and `far` 60 for the same reason. `VehicleCanvas` takes `interior` (an `InteriorSwatch`) and passes its `cabinLight` to the rig.

## Sunroof

The model has one sunroof glass mesh (`008_Exterior_glass_2sided_dark`, no window triangles in it) and matching openings in the roof paint and the headliner; there is no blind mesh. `sunroof.ts` `addSunroofPatch` (called once after fit/align) copies the upward-facing glass triangles (by world-space vertex normal) into two hidden meshes: the outer one gets a clone of the roof paint material (front side only, material name kept so the body paint follows colour changes) and the inner one the same triangles drawn back-side with a standard material named so the interior colour treats it as `panel` (same colour as the headliner). Do not build the inner one from the slab's downward triangles: there are only 117 of them (top 390), they cover part of the opening, and with them the patch rendered near black on cream and brown interiors. `VehicleCanvas` `sunroof={false}` hides the glass and shows the patches (`setSunroof`); default is the model as is. The rim/bar seen from above is not a drawn feature: the patch was ~1cm narrower than the roof paint's opening, so the dark parts behind showed through the slit (hiding those meshes' triangles damages the roof, which was tried). Each pane of the patch is therefore enlarged by 3cm per side (`enlargedPositions`, per connected piece, positions rewritten as floats because the quantised attribute would clip) and the outer pane has a polygon offset. Do NOT enlarge the inner pane much more: at 12cm it pokes through the roof curve and shows as dark shards from outside. What remains: on white/pearl small near-black slivers at the opening corners and a faint hairline, and from inside thin slits of sky along the headliner opening (it is ~13cm wider than the glass). `sunroofOpen` slides the glass back and _down_ so it hides under the roof paint (a +0.03 lift left it lying on top of the paint), and from 55% open it fades out and is hidden at the end, because the gap between paint and headliner is too thin to hide the tucked glass reliably (on a light background it showed through the headliner as a pale trapezoid) (`slideSunroof`: world offset (0, -0.09, -0.55) converted to the glass parent's local frame once in `addSunroofPatch`, eased over 0.9s in a `useFrame` in `GltfModel`, instant under reduced motion); the patches never move and the slide only matters when `sunroof` is true. Not done: a blind (there is no blind mesh, so open shows the headliner opening's rim and the sky).

## Brake caliper colours

`apply-three-caliper.ts` recolours the `Brake_Caliper` mesh (one paint material, base colour rgb(2,2,2), roughness 0, metal: it renders as a black mirror, so even `standard` is repainted silver-grey so the chip matches what you see). `VehicleCanvas` takes `caliper` (a `CaliperSwatch`). Car paint skips the wheel parts by name, so the two do not collide. The caliper is small (about 25x40 px behind the spokes at the default wheel-view zoom) and rendered colours are duller than their hex (yellow needs low metalness or the environment reflection turns it mustard); judge a new colour by looking at the wheel view, not by the hex.

## Interior colours

The interior meshes have no textures: shading is baked into vertex colours and the material base colour is near black (metalness 1, copper-looking when painted). `apply-three-interior.ts` clones the material as a `MeshPhysicalMaterial` (copy via `MeshStandardMaterial.prototype.copy`, never `MeshPhysicalMaterial.copy(standard)`, which throws on the missing clearcoat fields) with metalness 0, high roughness and low specular, and sets its colour; originals are kept in `userData` and restored. Parts are told apart by **mesh name** only (one mesh = one colour): `seat` seats, `panel` doors, door cards, centre console and the whole headliner, `shell` A/B/C pillars, rear quarter trim and the mirror housing, `wheel` the steering wheel, `dash` the whole dashboard. Not recolourable: vents, screen and cluster, floor and carpet, belts, trim strips (`*_none`). Impossible with this model: a two-tone dashboard, a headliner in another colour than the door cards, separate A and B pillar colours, cushion vs backrest. Colours render much lighter than their hex under the studio lighting, and the original black cabin needs _more_ light to read while light leather needs _less_, so each swatch carries its own `cabinLight`; if you add a swatch, tune `color` and `cabinLight` together by eye in both the seat and dashboard views (expose the scene on `window` temporarily and delete the hook). Garnish (carbon/wood/metal) and seat layout are not modelled. Camera/scene mutations live in module-level helpers in `inspect-controls.tsx` because the React Compiler lint rejects them in components. Find coordinates for a new preset by exposing the camera on `window` temporarily and reading bounding boxes of the named parts, then delete the hook.

## Model notes

Use only openly licensed models; no EULA-restricted meshes. The CC BY 4.0 notice (creator, source, license, warranty disclaimer, modifications, trademark) lives in `src/vehicles.ts` `credit` and is rendered by the host's credit component (Storybook: `VehicleCredit`) — keep it in sync when the model processing changes; the long form is `packages/ui/.storybook/public/models/README.md`. G05 doors, hood, and seats are half-meshes; `assembleG05Three` mirrors them on Z and hides `*_void` / light-effect nodes. Interior also loads non-color trim and the steering wheel. Dark windshield glass is made transmissive so the cabin is visible. Wheel size (`src/wheel-size.ts`): the model's rim measures 26.8cm radius (≈21in), so 21in is native; other sizes scale the rim in the wheel plane by `inch/21` (width, brakes and screws unchanged) and move the tire's inner radius by the same ratio while its outer radius (≈37.5cm) stays, recomputed from the stored native positions each time. The wheel spoke design is one shape. Paints carry a `clearcoat` (0 = matte). Paint skips the wheel parts (`Wheel_Tire/Rim/Screws/Connecter`, `Brake_*`) by mesh name even when their material is named like paint; panels such as wheel arches are still painted. Paint story: GSAP pin + yaw. `X5 원본`: OrbitControls (drag orbit, wheel zoom). Do not copy car meshes from third-party visualizers. Vision M NEXT print data is out of scope.
